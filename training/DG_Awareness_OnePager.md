# Data Governance — What Every Fund Services Colleague Needs to Know
### One-page awareness material for new joiners and business users

## Why this matters to you

If your role touches NAV, investor records, trade data, or FX rates, you are working with a **Critical Data Element (CDE)**. Errors in these fields can misstate a fund's NAV, breach a regulatory reporting obligation, or cause investor harm — so they're governed more tightly than ordinary data.

## Your three responsibilities

1. **Know if you touch a CDE.** Check the CDE Register for your domain — if a field you enter, edit, or approve is listed, you're part of its quality chain.
2. **Raise it, don't fix it silently.** If you spot a data quality issue (a stale price, a mismatched reconciliation, an unexplained variance), log it via the Issue Management process — do not just correct it and move on. Silent fixes hide systemic problems.
3. **Respect ownership.** Every CDE has a named Data Owner and Steward. If you need a change to how a CDE is defined, calculated, or sourced, that goes through them — not an ad hoc fix downstream.

## What "good" looks like

- You can name the Data Owner for the data you work with daily
- You know where to find the data catalogue entry for your key systems
- You've logged an issue before instead of quietly patching around it
- You understand why a Tier 1 CDE (like NAV per share) gets same-day escalation, while a Tier 2 reference field doesn't
